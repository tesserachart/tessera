# Indicator values

Every per-bar indicator a chart draws (SMA, EMA, WMA, VWAP, Volume, RSI, ATR, Bollinger, MACD, Stochastic, Delta and CVD, custom JavaScript indicators) is computed in the runtime and kept there, one frame per market, bar type and session filter (`docs/architecture.md`, Indicator values). The chart draws what `indicators.series` sends; reopening a chart, switching back to a timeframe or scrolling back reads the kept values instead of computing them again.

## Sub-features

- `ind-reopen` a chart reopened (a reload, or a workspace shown again) draws the same values at every bar as when they were first computed, answered from the runtime's frame.
- `ind-timeframe` switching to a timeframe and back draws the first timeframe's values again, without computing them.
- `ind-scrollback` scrolled back as far as the chart holds bars, every indicator has values on the older bars, the custom ones included; the runtime extends the frame when the chart's bars reach further back (`indicators.reach`).
- `ind-warm` a study is one `indicators.series` subscription for every chart that asks for the same market, bars, session, indicator and inputs. It stays held for 5 minutes after its last chart lets go (16 at most), so a remount, a dock reload or a second chart draws it at once. A reconnect keeps the subscription the client carries over and its values until the new snapshot replaces them; it is asked for again only after a gap, or when print bars moved while it was away. Proof: `node .agents/skills/verify-tessera/scripts/study-warm.ts [out dir] [ui dir] [tag]` starts its own sim runtime, puts CVD on NQ 1m, switches to the Journal and back, cuts the page's socket, then puts CVD on a second NQ 1m chart. It counts the CVD subscriptions on the wire (one on the first socket, one carried over by the reconnect, none let go) and samples the CVD legend every 100 ms (never without a value). On main before this change the reconnect alone subscribed it three times and the legend lost its value.
- `ind-cvd` the CVD pane shows its newest value as soon as the newest session's prints are read; older sessions fill in behind it, newest first.
- `ind-print-bars` on tick, range, volume, delta or renko bars every indicator is computed over those bars.
- `ind-opening-range` the library's `Opening range` (Volatility) adds `OR 15` to the chart: each session's 15-minute range (dashed, with a faint fill), its 60-minute initial balance and the ±50% / ±100% extensions of the balance once it closes, timed from the regular open. Every level is a plain horizontal line from its window's open to the session's end. 1m, 5m, 1h, 1D and 500-tick bars read the same levels, because the runtime measures them from the session's stored one-minute bars whenever the chart's own bars do not tile the window; on 1D each session draws as a tick across its bar. `indicators.compute '{"indicator":"opening_range","instrument":"NQZ6.CME","tf":"1h","limit":3}'` answers the same `orHigh`, `orLow`, `ibHigh`, `ibLow` as on `1m`. Proof: `node .agents/skills/verify-tessera/scripts/opening-range.ts [out dir] [ui dir] [tag]` starts its own sim runtime, adds the study from the library, shoots each interval and the settings, and writes `<tag>-levels.json` (legend and last values per interval); `cargo test -p tessera-indicators opening` and `every_interval_reads_the_opening_levels_one_minute_bars_measure` hold the numbers.
- `ind-legend-menu` a click on a legend row's name, or a right-click anywhere on the row, opens one menu under the name with `Settings…`, `Hide` (`Show` once hidden) and `Remove`, the same for indicators and the order-flow studies (Footprint, Volume profile, Big trades, CVD). `Settings…` opens the settings popover and it stays open. Hide and Remove are saved with `layout.save` and survive a reload; the chart's right-click `Layers` submenu lists every layer with its checkbox and a remove button.
- `ind-palette` a newly added indicator starts in the chart palette, never teal or brass: SMA t2, EMA t1, WMA t2 dashed, VWAP mist at 55%, RSI, ATR, MACD and %K mist with MACD's signal and %D in t3, histograms in up and down, the volume profile's POC line mist at 70%, the opening range's OR lines t1 dashed, its IB lines mist dashed and its extensions t4 dashed. A custom study that leaves a colour out gets mist, t2, t3, up, down for its lines in turn, mist at 12% for a fill and mist for a colour input. `layout.get` shows the layer's `style` with those swatch names; a layer saved before keeps the style it was saved with.

- `ind-editor-room` a custom indicator's editor wraps long lines, each wrapped part hanging under its own line's indent, so no line runs past the editor's edge (the `.cm-scroller`'s `scrollWidth` equals its `clientWidth`). `separator "Resize the code"` between the code and the preview drags, steps with the arrow keys and resets with Enter or a double-click; the share is kept across reloads. `button "Maximise the code"` (⌘⇧↵ while typing) hides the library, the preview and the inputs so the code takes the page; `button "Show the chart"` (or ⌘⇧↵ again) brings them back. Unit proof: `apps/desktop/test/indicator-editor.test.tsx`.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- A chart's legend: each indicator row reads its values at the bar under the pointer, or at the last bar. The row's name (`button "VP: menu"`) opens its menu; the gear (`button "VP: settings"`), eye and × at the row's end act directly, and `Delete` on a focused row removes it.
- The chart's ƒx focuses the Indicators panel, whose `Add to chart` adds them to the active chart; the timeframe group and the keyboard move the chart.
- The rail's `button "Indicators"`, ⌥4 or ⌘K `Go to Indicators` opens the Indicators page (`heading "Indicators"` over `region "Indicators"`): the same library, chart menu and `New indicator` as the panel, full page. It is a panel too: dragging that rail item onto the desk docks it there, and ⌘K `Show Indicators` or the Panels library opens it on the desk.

## Driving it

The e2e session's chart works, but timings and the same-bar comparison need a script: start the sim runtime by hand (`tessera-test-runtime serve --port <port> --ui-dir apps/desktop/dist --data-dir <tmp> --launch-tickets`, with `TESSERA_NO_RITHMIC=1`), add the indicators with `chart.add_layer` (`tileId` `chart-1`; a custom one after `indicators.create_custom` with `crates/tessera-indicators/js/ema_cross.js`), and drive Chromium with Playwright on `<origin>/#ticket=<ticket>` from the ready line.

- **Same bar, cached and computed.** Open the chart on a fresh runtime (every value computed), wait until its WebSocket is quiet, put the pointer at a fixed point of the chart and read each legend row (`.ind-legend [data-layer] .vals`). Reload the page (the runtime's frame answers) and read the same point; switch `1m` then `5m` and read it again. All three readings are identical.
- **Runtime.** `runtime.ts <port> indicators.compute '{"indicator":"ema","instrument":"NQZ6.CME","tf":"5m","limit":3}'` answers the chart's values with `computedFrom`, the first bar they are computed from.
- **Scroll back.** Click the chart, `Home`, `-` four times, `ArrowLeft` sixty times, pointer at the left of the plot: every row has values at once and the page sends no read for them.
- **Proof in tests.** `cargo test -p tessera-indicators` (`every_study_cached_equals_a_full_recompute` holds every study bit for bit to a full recompute under ticks, prepends, corrections and late prints; the cache tests cover each invalidation and the memory bound) and `bunx vitest run apps/desktop/test/studies.test.ts apps/desktop/test/flow-live.test.ts`; `apps/desktop/test/study-removal.test.tsx` holds the legend menu and the Layers menu to what `layout.save` stores.

## Gotchas

- The frozen market never ticks, so a frame's forming bar never changes there; live cadence needs the busy market.
- On the mock market a 1m frame reads every older session's prints in the background (about a minute for 30 sessions); the CVD's newest value does not wait for it.
- Legend rows of a custom indicator read its name from the catalog, which a fresh runtime gives a new id: compare values, not ids.
